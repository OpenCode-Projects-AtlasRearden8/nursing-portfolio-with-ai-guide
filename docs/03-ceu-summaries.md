# CEU Summaries

Continuing-education units are the rarest currency in a portfolio because they
are hard to *retell* — you remember taking the module, not what it said. A
**CEU summary** is a short, structured record that makes any course, module, or
conference session retrievable in one glance, forever.

CEU records are about *you*, so they are among the safest things in a portfolio —
the only PHI risk is a case study inside a course description. Summarize the
*lesson*, never the case.

> **Educational content only.** Whether a course counts toward renewal, how many
> contact hours a module is worth, and what your state board accepts is decided
> by your state board of nursing and your certifying body. Log what they say,
> not what a summary template implies.

## The summary card

Five fields, one paragraph, no excuses:

| Field | Filled example (sanitized) |
|---|---|
| **Title & provider** | "Sepsis recognition in adults" — online module, ~2 contact hours (per provider) |
| **Date taken** | 2026-03-14 |
| **Format** | Self-paced online, with a post-test |
| **3 things I learned** | (a) red-flag vitals trends beat single readings; (b) newest bundle emphasizes early-liberation practices; (c) when to escalate vs. recheck |
| **How it changed me** | Added a trending-assessment habit to my shift routine; gave me language for running the next mock scenario |

Keep "3 things I learned" to ≥3 bullet-sized phrases you could explain out loud
in an elevator. If you can't produce three, you were attending, not learning —
which is also useful information.

## The rolling log (works in any spreadsheet or Markdown table)

| Date | Title | Provider | Hours | Category | 1-line takeaway |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-03-14 | Sepsis recognition | Online CE provider | 2 | Clinical | Trend vitals, escalate on bundle criteria |
| 2026-04-02 | Delegation & scope | Online CE provider | 1 | Professional | Delegate the task, keep the assessment |
| 2026-05-20 | Precepting basics (unit course) | My facility's educator | 4 | Teaching | Ask what they noticed first; give one goal |

## The AI loop for CEU summaries

1. **Right after the course,** you write 3–5 messy bullet points from the
   handout/notes — sanitized as always ([01-phi-safety.md](01-phi-safety.md)).
2. **You prompt the AI** (see [07-ai-prompt-library.md](07-ai-prompt-library.md#3-ceu-summary-prompts))
   to turn bullets into a five-field card and a one-row log entry.
3. **AI returns** the structured summary, maybe a tighter "How it changed me."
4. **You verify:** hours match the certificate, provider name spelled correctly,
   date accurate. **The certificate is truth, not the AI.**

Worked prompt (yours to adapt):

> Here are my rough notes from a continuing-education course (no patient info).
> Turn them into: a 5-field summary card (Title & provider, Date, Format,
> 3 things I learned, How it changed me) plus a single-row log entry
> (Date, Title, Provider, Hours, Category, 1-line takeaway).
> Notes: 2 hr module on sepsis recognition from an online CE provider, taken
> this week; big idea was unifying trends over single readings, bundle criteria
> for escalation, and I want to add trend-assessment to my routine.

## Categories worth assigning

Use 4–5 that map to what your employer and board actually care about:

- **Clinical** (disease, procedures, safety bundles)
- **Professional practice** (ethics, delegation, documentation)
- **Leadership/teaching** (precepting, charge prep)
- **Technology** (EHR skills, informatics)
- **Wellbeing & resilience** (stress, fatigue, healthy shift patterns)

Two categories per year usually dominate; both counts and categories read well
when your annual review asks "what did you learn this year?" — see
[06-annual-review.md](06-annual-review.md).

## Anti-patterns

- Logging only when renewal season forces you. A 5-minute ritual after each
  course keeps the log real.
- Copying the course abstract as your takeaway — you should sound like a person
  who *did* the training, not a brochure.
- Inventing hours, saving a screenshot of the certificate and calling it done —
  file the PDF AND a card; screenshots of certificates belong in your own
  private records folder, not in a portfolio you share.
- Putting real case details from a course's example patient into your card.
  Summarize the concept, not the case.
