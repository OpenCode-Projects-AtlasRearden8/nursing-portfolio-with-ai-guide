# Annual-Review Talking Points

Annual review season: the one hour a year where a portfolio matters most. This
chapter turns your sanitized competency sheets, CEU log, feedback cards, and
credential tracker into a short, confident, personal script — so you walk in
prepared, not braced.

> **Educational content only.** Every facility runs its own review form,
> rating language, and compensation process. What's here is *prep material* —
> you bring the substance; HR brings the form.

## The 4-layer structure

A clear review pitch fits in four layers, 90 seconds each:

1. **Growth** — one thing you got better at, with evidence.
2. **Contribution** — one thing you did for the unit or team (not just assigned work).
3. **Learning** — what you put into your own head this year (CEU categories here, not numerals).
4. **Next** — one specific ask and one specific goal, calendar-grounded.

Keep it tight: four bullets, four sentences of spice, done. Restraint is what
makes the powerful point — "this is a curated year, not a pile."

## Layer-by-layer builder

### Layer 1 — Growth

Straight from competency-sheet level-ups ([02-competency-sheets.md](02-competency-sheets.md)):

> "At review time last year I listed precepting students as a goal. This year
> I ran two shifts of student orientation under supervision, and my preceptor's
> notation on that moved to 'can teach.' "

Notice the shape: *goal → evidence → verified by a real record.* No patient
story; no guesstimate.

### Layer 2 — Contribution

Sanitized team-level contributions (unit-level, not person-level):

> "I rebuilt our shift-change report template and the unit adopted it; our
> audit showed noticeably fewer repeated follow-up calls at handoff."

Avoid "I covered for X when Y was on leave" phrasing if it names people. Say
"covered extra shifts during Q2 vacation season" — same fact, zero names.

### Layer 3 — Learning

Straight from CEU categories ([03-ceu-summaries.md](03-ceu-summaries.md)):

> "I focused my CEU on two categories this year: precepting (16 hrs) and
> informatics/EHR skills (8 hrs). The informatics hours helped me become our
> unit's go-to for the new documentation upgrade."

This is where the sanitized CEU log does the heavy lifting. Categories say
*vacation-styled learning*; counts say *I track my own growth.*

### Layer 4 — Next (the ask)

One ask that needs a resource/decision, one goal that needs only you:

> "Ask: two clinical days for the UGPIV course. Goal: finish UGPIV sign-off and
> take a charge-nurse rotation shift each quarter."

The goal should be something your credential tracker
([05-credential-tracker.md](05-credential-tracker.md)) and CEU log can corroborate.

## Prompt strategy: from docs to script

The AI turns your sanitized material into a draft script. The prompts (all in
[07-ai-prompt-library.md](07-ai-prompt-library.md#5-annual-review-prompts)) take:

1. Your competency sheet updates (sanitized).
2. Your CEU category totals (from your own log).
3. Your feedback cards merged by theme (see [04-preceptor-feedback.md](04-preceptor-feedback.md)).
4. Your single ask and single goal.

And return: a 4-layer script, a one-page "backup packet" (evidence table), and
a kinder-sounding ask line. You verify — *especially the numbers* — and switch
any sentence into your own voice.

> **Rule:** the script must sound like you talking to a manager you respect.
> If any sentence feels like borrowed management speak, rewrite–simplify.

## The backup packet (one page — keep it in a folder)

On one sheet (or one doc window) have ready, at most:

| Topic | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Growth claim | Competency sheet(s), level-up note |
| Contribution claim | Metric the unit audit showed, adopted template |
| Learning claim | CEU log lines with totals |
| Next goal | 1 line, with date |
| Credentials | Tracker rows (abbreviated) |
| Feedback trend | 3 bullets from preceptor feedback themes |

This is the "I did my homework" table. Nobody reads it all — its existence
changes the tone of the meeting.

## Anti-patterns

- Rehearsing to word-perfect. You want a *loaded rucksack*, not a speech.
- Bringing patient stories as evidence. The sanitized metric beats the case.
- Over-vowing. One ask, one goal, calendar-checked. Promising 6 things and
  doing 2 reads worse than promising 2 and doing 2.
- Being shy with the ask. If you don't ask, everything you say becomes its
  justification — say the ask clearly.
