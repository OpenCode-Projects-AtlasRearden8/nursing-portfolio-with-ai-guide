Do not include PHI

Before any content goes into a portfolio file, note, or AI prompt:

  • Never include patient identifiers — Names, initials, MRNs, dates of birth, room/bed numbers.
  • Watch for identifying combinations — A rare procedure + a date + a unit can point at one specific patient.
  • Use roles instead of names — “my preceptor,” “charge nurse” — never “Jane R. on 4 West.”
  • No screenshots or photos of work — EHR screenshots and printed MARs violate privacy outright.
  • No secrets, ever — Logins, badge numbers, API keys stay out of files and prompts.

Full rules: PHI Safety.

AI Prompt Library

Copy-paste starters for every chapter of this manual. Each prompt is sanitizer-safe by design: it only ever carries your already- sanitized notes, never PHI (see 01-phi-safety.md).

How to use any prompt:

  1. Sanitize your raw notes first. Names/patients/dates-of-battle never survive into the prompt.
  2. Paste the prompt below into your AI chat (any mainstream assistant, or your own locally-run model — nothing here needs to leave your machine).
  3. Verify the output against a real source before you file it. AI drafts; your certificate, your check-off sheet, your preceptor's actual sentence are the records that count.
  4. File the final version with the chapter it belongs to.

A shared line to append to almost every prompt:

No patient information is included in anything I paste — keep it that way.

1. Getting started prompts

Set the assistant's job (paste once, at the top of a chat):

You are my writing helper for a professional nursing portfolio. I am a nurse organizing competency sheets, CEU summaries, preceptor feedback, credentials, and annual-review talking points. Style: clear, honest, plain English, no medical-jargon padding, no invented facts. If I give you numbers, use them as-is and tell me if anything looks off. Never add facts I didn't give you. Assume everything I paste is already sanitized — and if I paste anything that looks like patient information, stop and tell me to sanitize first.

Data hygiene check (run before most sessions):

Here is what I plan to paste into a later message. Check it for anything that identifies a patient, coworker, or facility and tell me only what to remove or generalize. Paste: [your raw notes]

2. Competency-sheet prompts

Build a sheet from rough notes:

Turn these rough notes into a one-page competency sheet with these sections: Skill name, Category, Level (Introduced / Practicing / Independent / Experienced / Can teach), Verified by, Context, Strengths, Growth edge, Next step, Last updated. Keep my phrasing when it's already good. If a level I claimed sounds inconsistent with the notes, ask me rather than guess. Notes: [paste]

Reflection-tightening:

Here is the reflection part of a competency sheet: [paste]. Tighten it to two sentences, first person, honest tone, no buzzwords, no patient story. It should say what's solid and what I'm actively working on.

Level ladder sanity check:

I described this skill as [paste level + context]. Does that level wording match what I described? If not, suggest which of these fits better and why: Introduced, Practicing, Independent, Experienced, Can teach.

3. CEU-summary prompts

Course notes → summary card + log row:

Here are my rough notes from a continuing-education course (sanitized, no patient info). Turn them into: (a) a five-field summary card — Title & provider, Date taken, Format, "3 things I learned," "How it changed me"; and (b) a single-row log entry: Date, Title, Provider, Hours, Category, 1-line takeaway. Keep each "thing I learned" to one line I could say out loud. Do not invent hours or a provider name I didn't give you. Notes: [paste]

Theme my CEU year:

From this CEU log, group my hours by category and tell me which two categories dominated my year. One short paragraph, no flattery. Log: [paste your log table]

Compare to category plan:

My category targets for this year: [categories + hour goals]. Here's my log to date: [paste]. Where am I on track, behind, or done? Plain table.

4. Preceptor-feedback prompts

Cluster feedback cards:

Here are my short preceptor-feedback cards from the past [N] weeks (sanitized — roles not names, no case details). Cluster them into 2–4 strengths and one recurring growth theme. Quote at most once, briefly. Cards: [paste]

Draft a preceptorship summary:

Using these clustered feedback notes, draft a one-page preceptorship summary in first person, four numbered points, no patient-specific content, no names. Tone: calm, factual, the kind of paragraph a manager who respects me would recognize. Notes: [paste] Notes: [paste]

Turn awkward wording into a good ask:

I want to say to my preceptor: "[your awkward draft]." Make it one calm, short request I would actually say out loud, asking for feedback I can file in my growth record. Give two versions: casual and slightly more formal.

5. Annual-review prompts

Build the 4-layer script:

Using the following sanitized material — competency updates [paste], CEU category totals [paste], feedback themes [paste], my one ask [paste], and my one goal [paste] — draft a four-part annual-review script, about 90 seconds each: Growth, Contribution, Learning, Next. Keep the ask concrete and one-sentence. No patient stories, no names, no invented metrics. If a claim has no evidence behind it in what I pasted, flag it instead of keeping it.

Backup packet:

Turn the same material into a one-page evidence table with rows: Growth, Contribution, Learning, Next, Credentials, Feedback trend; columns: Claim / Supporting evidence. Short cells, table format.

Kinder, firmer ask line:

Rewrite my ask — "[paste]" — so it is polite but unmistakably a request, with a clear what and a clear when. Two versions.

6. Utility prompts

Sanitizer (use constantly):

Scrub this text so it cannot identify a patient, coworker, or specific facility, keeping only skill, setting-type, and lesson. Show me before/after and list what you removed. Text: [paste]

Plain-English rewrite for renewal pages:

Rewrite this renewal-requirement text in plain English a busy nurse can act on: [paste text from the official issuer page]

Backward planning:

My credential expires on DATE and the renewal takes about [N] hours. Give me three dates: when to do the desk work, when to finish, and when to set a reminder. Show the steps between each.

Frequency of use (suggested rhythm)

cadence prompts you run
After any course (5 min)#3 card + log row
Each shift block with feedback (2 min)#4 card or cluster
Each new/level-up skill (10 min)#2 sheet + reflection
Quarterly desk ritual (20 min)#5 review-draft (first pass) + #6 backward plan
Annual review prep (one evening)#5 script + packet + ask line

Nothing here is clever. That's the point — small, repeatable, sanitized loops that compound into a real portfolio.
