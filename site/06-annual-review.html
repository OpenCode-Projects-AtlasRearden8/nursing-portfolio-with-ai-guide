Do not include PHI

Before any content goes into a portfolio file, note, or AI prompt:

  • Never include patient identifiers — Names, initials, MRNs, dates of birth, room/bed numbers.
  • Watch for identifying combinations — A rare procedure + a date + a unit can point at one specific patient.
  • Use roles instead of names — “my preceptor,” “charge nurse” — never “Jane R. on 4 West.”
  • No screenshots or photos of work — EHR screenshots and printed MARs violate privacy outright.
  • No secrets, ever — Logins, badge numbers, API keys stay out of files and prompts.

Full rules: PHI Safety.

Annual-Review Talking Points

Annual review season: the one hour a year where a portfolio matters most. This chapter turns your sanitized competency sheets, CEU log, feedback cards, and credential tracker into a short, confident, personal script — so you walk in prepared, not braced.

Educational content only. Every facility runs its own review form, rating language, and compensation process. What's here is prep material — you bring the substance; HR brings the form.

The 4-layer structure

A clear review pitch fits in four layers, 90 seconds each:

  1. Growth — one thing you got better at, with evidence.
  2. Contribution — one thing you did for the unit or team (not just assigned work).
  3. Learning — what you put into your own head this year (CEU categories here, not numerals).
  4. Next — one specific ask and one specific goal, calendar-grounded.

Keep it tight: four bullets, four sentences of spice, done. Restraint is what makes the powerful point — "this is a curated year, not a pile."

Layer-by-layer builder

Layer 1 — Growth

Straight from competency-sheet level-ups (02-competency-sheets.md):

"At review time last year I listed precepting students as a goal. This year I ran two shifts of student orientation under supervision, and my preceptor's notation on that moved to 'can teach.' "

Notice the shape: goal → evidence → verified by a real record. No patient story; no guesstimate.

Layer 2 — Contribution

Sanitized team-level contributions (unit-level, not person-level):

"I rebuilt our shift-change report template and the unit adopted it; our audit showed noticeably fewer repeated follow-up calls at handoff."

Avoid "I covered for X when Y was on leave" phrasing if it names people. Say "covered extra shifts during Q2 vacation season" — same fact, zero names.

Layer 3 — Learning

Straight from CEU categories (03-ceu-summaries.md):

"I focused my CEU on two categories this year: precepting (16 hrs) and informatics/EHR skills (8 hrs). The informatics hours helped me become our unit's go-to for the new documentation upgrade."

This is where the sanitized CEU log does the heavy lifting. Categories say vacation-styled learning; counts say I track my own growth.

Layer 4 — Next (the ask)

One ask that needs a resource/decision, one goal that needs only you:

"Ask: two clinical days for the UGPIV course. Goal: finish UGPIV sign-off and take a charge-nurse rotation shift each quarter."

The goal should be something your credential tracker (05-credential-tracker.md) and CEU log can corroborate.

Prompt strategy: from docs to script

The AI turns your sanitized material into a draft script. The prompts (all in 07-ai-prompt-library.md) take:

  1. Your competency sheet updates (sanitized).
  2. Your CEU category totals (from your own log).
  3. Your feedback cards merged by theme (see 04-preceptor-feedback.md).
  4. Your single ask and single goal.

And return: a 4-layer script, a one-page "backup packet" (evidence table), and a kinder-sounding ask line. You verify — especially the numbers — and switch any sentence into your own voice.

Rule: the script must sound like you talking to a manager you respect. If any sentence feels like borrowed management speak, rewrite–simplify.

The backup packet (one page — keep it in a folder)

On one sheet (or one doc window) have ready, at most:

Topic Evidence
Growth claimCompetency sheet(s), level-up note
Contribution claimMetric the unit audit showed, adopted template
Learning claimCEU log lines with totals
Next goal1 line, with date
CredentialsTracker rows (abbreviated)
Feedback trend3 bullets from preceptor feedback themes

This is the "I did my homework" table. Nobody reads it all — its existence changes the tone of the meeting.

Anti-patterns

  • Rehearsing to word-perfect. You want a loaded rucksack, not a speech.
  • Bringing patient stories as evidence. The sanitized metric beats the case.
  • Over-vowing. One ask, one goal, calendar-checked. Promising 6 things and doing 2 reads worse than promising 2 and doing 2.
  • Being shy with the ask. If you don't ask, everything you say becomes its justification — say the ask clearly.
