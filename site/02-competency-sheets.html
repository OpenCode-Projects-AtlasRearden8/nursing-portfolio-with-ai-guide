Do not include PHI

Before any content goes into a portfolio file, note, or AI prompt:

  • Never include patient identifiers — Names, initials, MRNs, dates of birth, room/bed numbers.
  • Watch for identifying combinations — A rare procedure + a date + a unit can point at one specific patient.
  • Use roles instead of names — “my preceptor,” “charge nurse” — never “Jane R. on 4 West.”
  • No screenshots or photos of work — EHR screenshots and printed MARs violate privacy outright.
  • No secrets, ever — Logins, badge numbers, API keys stay out of files and prompts.

Full rules: PHI Safety.

Competency Sheets

A competency sheet is a one-page, sanitized record of one skill: what it is, what you can do with it, who verified it, and where your gaps are. Over a year these sheets become your strongest annual-review evidence — and the raw material for resignation letters, transfer requests, and a future resume.

Educational content only. Facility check-off requirements, sim-lab passes, and super-user designations are set by your institution and accreditor. This manual shows you how to document and talk about competencies; your unit's own checklists outrank these templates.

What makes a good competency sheet

Element Example (sanitized)
Name of the skillPeripheral IV insertion, adult
CategoryProcedural / assessment / equipment / education
LevelIntroduced / practiced with supervision / independent / can teach others
VerificationWho and what: "annual skills fair check-off," "preceptor sign-off," "simulation lab"
ContextSetting type and rough frequency: "metro med-surg, ~2–4 per shift"
ReflectionWhat's solid, what's next, one concrete growth step
No PHINo patients, no dates that trace, no unit identifiers

The reflection is the part everyone skips and the part managers actually read. Two honest sentences beat a wall of "competent at everything."

Filled example

## Competency: Peripheral IV insertion (adult)

- **Category:** Procedural
- **Level:** Independent (can orient students with supervision)
- **Verified by:** Annual skills fair, plus consistent preceptor feedback
- **Context:** Adult med-surg; routine workload, frequent difficult-access patients
- **Strengths:** High first-stick success on routine access; calm with anxious patients
- **Growth edge:** Ultrasound-guided PIV — observed many times, not yet signed off
- **Next step:** Schedule UGPIV module with unit educator by end of next quarter
- **Last updated:** 2026-02 (I keep my own local updated date — no patient data here)

Levels you can use (plain English)

  • Introduced: watched, technically capable, not yet trusted solo on it
  • Practicing: do it routinely with support available
  • Independent: do it without a check; normal part of my shift
  • Experienced: handle the complicated versions and troubleshoot for others
  • Can teach: oriented students/staff on it (say so only if true)

Pick honestly. "Practicing with supervision" reads as confident, not weak — managers trust self-assessments that aren't all green.

The AI loop for competency sheets

  1. You capture raw notes (sanitized): skill, level, verification contexts, one honest reflection.
  2. You prompt the AI to restructure. Several ready prompts are in 07-ai-prompt-library.md.
  3. AI returns a formatted sheet, a tighter reflection, or a growth plan.
  4. You verify against your skills-fair record/preceptor sign-off, sanitize once more against 01-phi-safety.md, then file.

Example raw note → AI prompt (both already safe to type):

Here are my rough notes for a competency sheet (no patient info included): IV insertion adult, independent, skills-fair check-off last year, pretty good at nervous patients, weak on ultrasound-guided. Turn this into a one-page competency sheet with the categories: Category, Level, Verified by, Context, Strengths, Growth edge, Next step, Last updated.

Anti-patterns

  • Completing the sheet with a patient story instead of a reflection. Write the lesson, not the case.
  • Copy-pasting a facility checklist whose wording you don't have permission to redistribute — paraphrase in your own words instead.
  • Listing a skill at a level you can't back up if asked. Verification column exists precisely so every claim has a paper trail.
  • Letting the sheet go stale. 20 minutes per quarter keeps a healthy one alive.
