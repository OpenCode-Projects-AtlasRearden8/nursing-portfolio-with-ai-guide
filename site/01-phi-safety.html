Do not include PHI

Before any content goes into a portfolio file, note, or AI prompt:

  • Never include patient identifiers — Names, initials, MRNs, dates of birth, room/bed numbers.
  • Watch for identifying combinations — A rare procedure + a date + a unit can point at one specific patient.
  • Use roles instead of names — “my preceptor,” “charge nurse” — never “Jane R. on 4 West.”
  • No screenshots or photos of work — EHR screenshots and printed MARs violate privacy outright.
  • No secrets, ever — Logins, badge numbers, API keys stay out of files and prompts.

PHI Safety: What Never Goes In Your Portfolio

The do-not-include-PHI sidebar. This page is the safety gate for the whole manual. Read it before you touch your first competency sheet, CEU summary, or annual-review note — and re-scan it before you share anything with an AI tool, a preceptor, or a manager.

The single most important word in the table is combination. A single detail (rare procedure, specific date, tiny unit) is usually harmless. Two or three combined can point at exactly one human being. When sanitizing, it is almost never one field you remove — it is the overlap between fields.

Pre-flight sanitization checklist

Run your entry through these before it goes into a prompt, a portfolio file, or a review folder:

  1. Names gone? No patient/coworker/facility names you don't need. Say "a long-term ICU patient," "my preceptor," "my mid-size community hospital."
  2. Dates generalized? "A winter night shift" instead of "Jan 14." Exact dates are fine for your own CEU certificates and renewals — that's your record, not a patient's story.
  3. Numbers depersonalized? Room numbers out. Counts about you are fine ("I did 40 IV starts across the year"); counts about a patient are not.
  4. Combination test? Could a coworker recognize which patient this is? If yes, blur one more axis: the procedure, the date, the unit, or the outcome.
  5. Images stripped? No screenshots or photos unless fully synthetic (your own mock table).
  6. AI-prompt-safety double-check? Prompts only ever carry sanitized, already-blurred summaries. If you can't sanitize it, you can't prompt it.

Worked example: sanitizing an anecdote

Before (do not use):

On Tuesday night I had R.T., MRN 44718—, a 68-year-old in room 12 who went for a Whipple that afternoon, and at 0200 his Jackson-Pratt drained over 200 mL of blood; Dr. Mati came and we packed him, and my preceptor Kay coached me through hanging 4 units.

After (sanitized, portfolio-safe):

During a night shift, I cared for a postoperative patient whose surgical drain output changed suddenly. I notified the provider, supported fluid resuscitation per protocol, and my preceptor coached me through IV push medications I had not previously administered. What I took from it: report drain output changes early; a calm, scripted SBAR gets a faster response.

Notice what survived: the skill, the setting level, the lesson. What got removed: the person, the date, the room, the colleague, the institution.

Test to apply: if the before-version makes you think "oh, that was Mr. —" you are done sanitizing; the after-version is what belongs in the portfolio.

How this maps to the rest of the manual

  • Competency sheets (02) — describe the skill, never the patient who taught you it.
  • CEU summaries (03) — CEU records are about you; they almost never touch PHI at all.
  • Preceptor feedback (04) — feedback talks about your practice, not cases; store learned lessons, not story details.
  • Annual review (06) — talking points come from the same sanitized set; you'll never need a patient story to sound competent.

The one-line rule

If it would be awkward to explain to a privacy officer, don't put it in the portfolio — and definitely don't paste it into a chat window.
