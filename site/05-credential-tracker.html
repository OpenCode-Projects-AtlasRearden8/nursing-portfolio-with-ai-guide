Do not include PHI

Before any content goes into a portfolio file, note, or AI prompt:

  • Never include patient identifiers — Names, initials, MRNs, dates of birth, room/bed numbers.
  • Watch for identifying combinations — A rare procedure + a date + a unit can point at one specific patient.
  • Use roles instead of names — “my preceptor,” “charge nurse” — never “Jane R. on 4 West.”
  • No screenshots or photos of work — EHR screenshots and printed MARs violate privacy outright.
  • No secrets, ever — Logins, badge numbers, API keys stay out of files and prompts.

Full rules: PHI Safety.

Credential Tracker

The credential tracker is the one-page dashboard of everything you're licensed or certified to do, and when it expires. It's the least AI-flavored chapter of this manual — the data comes from certificates and regulatory mail — but AI still earns its keep here: as a renewal-checklist generator, a reminder-keeper, and a plain-English explainer of renewal rules.

Educational content only. Your state board, your certification body, and your facility's education department own this truth. Never let a chatbot's recital of "renewal requirements" stand in for looking at the actual renewal page. Treat every AI answer about credentials as a summary that must be checked against the source.

Status colors (use in your file, plain text is fine): Green (>6 months out), Yellow (3–6), Orange (1–3), Red (lapsed or <1 month). Seeing a column of color names is faster than doing date math.

The renewal protocol (any credential, same 5 steps)

  1. Pull the source page. Find the issuer's official renewal page. Screenshot to your own private location if it helps. Never rely on memory or copy someone else's summary.
  2. Write the requirement in plain English. One sentence: what, how many hours, what form, what fee. That sentence's wording is yours — it is the one thing LLMs can help rewrite.
  3. Backward-plan 3 dates. (a) date the renewal is due, (b) date you must start the course/exam process to finish in time (usually 8–12 weeks earlier), (c) date to do the desk work (70+ days before due).
  4. Record it in this table. Add "Next action + date."
  5. Set the calendar on the device you actually look at (phone, wall calendar — whichever you will not ignore).

Guardrail: pasted text about your own credentials is the safest prompt input the whole manual has to offer (it's about you, not patients). But no — don't paste your full license number or SSN into a chat. Redact "RN-000000X" to "RN-XXXXX00X" (keep just enough shape to recognize); your transcript is still usable.

Quarterly desk ritual (20 minutes)

  • &#9744; Add/update every credential's row — including days
  • &#9744; Recompute CEU totals year-to-date from the log in 03-ceu-summaries.md
  • &#9744; Verify any newly earned cert physically arrived (not just submitted)
  • &#9744; Anything Yellow? Book the course now, not next quarter
  • &#9744; One line "Notes" per row on any weirdness (fee changed, provider moved, prerequisite added)

That's the entire lifetime maintenance of a credential tracker. Under 30 minutes, four times a year.

Anti-patterns

  • Replacing "issuer" with a nickname ("that nurse board thing") — a loss of of precision you will regret in renewal season.
  • Storing your license number repeatedly in shared docs. Keep it in your private record at home; "RN license" is enough of a label here.
  • Trusting any chatbot on "does this course count toward X" — there is exactly one source of truth and it is not a language model.
  • Letting the tracker drift because "the emails will remind me." They will not arrive at the pace you need.
