Do not include PHI

Before any content goes into a portfolio file, note, or AI prompt:

  • Never include patient identifiers — Names, initials, MRNs, dates of birth, room/bed numbers.
  • Watch for identifying combinations — A rare procedure + a date + a unit can point at one specific patient.
  • Use roles instead of names — “my preceptor,” “charge nurse” — never “Jane R. on 4 West.”
  • No screenshots or photos of work — EHR screenshots and printed MARs violate privacy outright.
  • No secrets, ever — Logins, badge numbers, API keys stay out of files and prompts.

Read Me First

Professional Portfolio With AI Help — a healthcare-education manual for registered nurses (and other healthcare workers) who want to build a working, hospital-safe professional portfolio with the help of AI tools.

This manual is educational content, never medical advice. It teaches you how to document, organize, and talk about your own professional work. It does not teach clinical care, does not interpret your license or credentials, and cannot make decisions for you. Your nursing policy manual, your state board of nursing, your HR department, and your credentialed preceptor/manager always outrank anything written here. If any advice in this manual conflicts with your facility's policy, follow your facility's policy.

The three rules of this manual

  1. Never put PHI (protected health information) in your portfolio, your prompts, or this project's files. See 01-phi-safety.md. This includes anything that could identify a patient, coworker, or facility — that means no names, no room numbers, no MRNs, no "you know the guy on 4 West" stories. Cartoonishly specific clinical anecdotes are out; sanitized "skill + setting + what I learned" summaries are in.
  2. Your portfolio describes you, not a patient. Every competency sheet, CEU summary, and talking point should pass one test: could I read this out loud in a hallway with the unit clerk, the charge nurse, and a visitor listening without anyone learning anything about a patient? If not, rewrite it.
  3. AI drafts, you verify. AI is great at restructuring your rough notes into a polished sheet. AI is not a source of truth for your license number, your CEU totals, your facility's policy, or anything clinical. Every AI-generated sentence about your career gets checked against a real document before it goes in the portfolio.

What NOT to include (the short list)

Even sanitized entries must stay scrubbed of these. See 01-phi-safety.md for the full sidebar and examples.

  • Patient names or initials, MRNs, room/bed numbers, dates of birth
  • Any combination of details that could point to one specific patient
  • Coworker or preceptor names (use roles: "my ICU preceptor," "charge nurse")
  • Exact facility names where a story could land on someone (use "a metro ICU" or keep "my facility" — your employer knows where you work, they don't need your notes to prove it)
  • Badge/ID numbers, internal logins, screenshots of the EHR, printed work labels
  • Your own identifiers you don't need here (SSN, home address)

How to use this with AI (the friendly version)

This manual assumes you use a conversational AI assistant (a chat app, or a local model you run yourself) as a writing helper — like a friend with a red pen who never gets tired of restructuring a table.

The pattern is always the same, in every chapter:

  1. You collect the raw, sanitized facts from your own records.
  2. You paste a sanitizer-checked summary into a prompt from 07-ai-prompt-library.md.
  3. The AI reorganizes, tightens wording, formats a table, or drafts the summary.
  4. You verify every factual claim against the real source, sanitize again, and only then file it in your portfolio (Markdown file, printed sheet, or however your unit stores these).

That's it. No magic. The manual just applies that loop to six different portfolio jobs.

Privacy of this project itself

Everything in this repository is template content — no real people, no real records, no secrets, no API keys. The build runs entirely on your machine with Python's standard library; nothing is sent anywhere. Load your own sanitized data before you start filling it in.
